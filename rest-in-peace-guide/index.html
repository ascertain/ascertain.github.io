Rest in Peace

— A compassionate guide for families in Sweden —

A practical step-by-step guide for Indian nationals and families living in Sweden. Covers Swedish authorities, Indian Embassy, cremation, repatriation, estate administration, insurance, religious rituals, and more.

⚠ Disclaimer: This guide is for general informational purposes only. It is not legal advice. Always verify current requirements directly with the relevant authorities. Fees, rules, and processes may change. Last reviewed: August 2026.

1. What To Do Immediately

A. If the person dies at home

If the death was expected (e.g. serious illness)

  • Contact the healthcare provider / home healthcare team responsible for the person.
  • A doctor must come and formally confirm the death.
  • The doctor handles the required death documentation.
  • The body is normally then taken care of by a funeral home.

Source: 1177 Vårdguiden — När någon har dött

If the death was unexpected or suspicious

  • Call 112 (police/ambulance).
  • Do not move or disturb the body unnecessarily.
  • Police may need to investigate before the body can be released.

B. If the person dies in hospital

The hospital will normally:

  • Have a doctor confirm the death.
  • Prepare the necessary medical / death documentation.
  • Notify Skatteverket.
  • Help the family say goodbye.
  • Help contact a funeral home, chaplain, or faith representative if requested.

Skatteverket registers the death once it receives the dödsbevis from the doctor/hospital. Source: Skatteverket

2. Swedish Registration of the Death

The doctor/hospital sends the dödsbevis (death certificate) to Skatteverket. The family does not normally need to do this themselves.

Dödsfallsintyg med släktutredning

After Skatteverket registers the death, the family can obtain this important document. It shows:

  • Date of death
  • Spouse / partner
  • Children
  • Relevant family information

This is commonly required by banks, insurance companies, pension providers, and other authorities.

Source: Skatteverket — När en anhörig dör

3. Contact a Funeral / Cremation Company

Contact a begravningsbyrå (funeral home) as soon as possible. They can help with:

  • Transportation of the deceased
  • Viewing / open viewing
  • Funeral ceremony (including Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Christian, or secular)
  • Cremation or burial
  • Cremation certificate
  • Ashes / urn handling
  • Contact with crematorium
  • All documentation

Cremation rules in Sweden

  • Skatteverket must issue an intyg (certificate) before cremation or burial can take place. The funeral home normally handles this application.
  • Cremation or burial should happen within 1 month of death.
  • After cremation, ashes should be buried/interred within 1 year.
  • Extensions can be requested from Skatteverket for special circumstances.

Source: Skatteverket — Begravning

4. If the Person Is an Indian Citizen

There are two parallel processes:

Swedish process

The death is first registered in Sweden and the Swedish death certificate is obtained. The full Swedish process (funeral, estate, bank, insurance) still applies because the death occurred in Sweden.

Indian Embassy process

The death must also be registered with the Embassy of India, Stockholm.

Documents required by the Indian Embassy

  1. Completed Miscellaneous / Attestation form
  2. Original passport of the deceased
  3. Original passport of the applicant
  4. Copy of deceased's passport
  5. Self-attested copy of applicant's passport
  6. Original death certificate from local authorities / Skatteverket + copy
  7. Proof of payment of SEK 224
  8. Return envelope if documents are being returned by post

The Embassy currently states same-day processing when the application is complete.

Source: Embassy of India — Death Certificate

Embassy emergency number for death cases: +46 73 484 4992
Consular contact: +46 8 684 32100
Source: Embassy of India — Contact

5. Cremation in Sweden & Taking Ashes to India

If the family decides to cremate in Sweden and carry the ashes to India, the process is:

Death → Swedish death registration → Funeral home → Skatteverket cremation certificate → Cremation → Cremation certificate → Indian Embassy → Ashes to India

Documents required by the Embassy for taking ashes

  • Swedish / local Death Certificate
  • Cremation Certificate
  • Certificate from the undertaker confirming the urn contains the deceased's ashes and is sealed
  • Original passport / OCI of deceased + copy
  • Passport / OCI of person carrying the ashes + copy
  • Embassy payment proof

The Embassy issues a letter to facilitate customs and other authorities for transporting the ashes to India.

Source: Embassy of India — Taking Ashes to India

6. Sending the Body to India (Repatriation)

IMPORTANT: If there is even a possibility that the family wants the body sent to India, tell the hospital/funeral home immediately. Do not approve cremation until the family has made a final decision.

Steps

  1. Tell the hospital/funeral home: "The family wants to repatriate the body to India. Please do not proceed with cremation."
  2. Appoint a funeral home experienced in international repatriation.
  3. Contact the Embassy of India, Stockholm.
  4. Contact all insurance providers and employer/HR before paying — some policies may cover repatriation.
  5. The funeral home coordinates with the airline/cargo company and Indian receiving funeral service.

Documents normally required

  • Swedish death certificate / dödsbevis
  • Deceased person's passport
  • Indian Embassy documentation / approval
  • Medical certificate
  • Smittointyg — certificate confirming no infectious disease
  • Balsameringsintyg — embalming certificate
  • Transportintyg — transport certificate
  • Lödningsintyg — certificate for the sealed coffin
  • Permission from Swedish authorities (issued by Skatteverket)
  • Airline cargo documentation
  • Documentation required by receiving authorities in India

Sources: Swedish Funeral Directors Association (SFIA), Norden.org

Check insurance BEFORE paying. Ask: "Does the deceased have any insurance that covers repatriation of mortal remains to India?" Check employer group life insurance, travel insurance, home insurance, credit-card insurance, private life insurance, pension/occupational insurance, and union insurance.

8. Bank Account

DO NOT:
  • Use the deceased person's debit card.
  • Withdraw money for personal use.
  • Transfer the deceased's money to another person's account.
  • Empty the account.
The money belongs to the dödsbo (estate), not automatically to the spouse.
DO: Contact the bank as soon as possible. Say: "The account holder has passed away. We need an appointment to understand how the estate can pay urgent bills and how the surviving family can access funds legally."

The bank can explain: accounts, mortgage, loans, joint accounts, autogiro, funeral payments, estate payments, insurance, what documents the spouse needs, and how the estate account works.

Source: Skatteverket

9. Insurance — Contact Immediately

Contact all of these during the first few days:

  • Life insurance
  • Home insurance
  • Accident insurance
  • Employer insurance
  • Pension insurance
  • Union insurance (if applicable)
  • Credit / mortgage insurance
  • Any private insurance in India

Ask for written confirmation of: policy number, beneficiary, amount payable, required documents, and expected processing time.

10. Employer / HR

If the person was employed, contact HR immediately. Ask them to check:

  • Group life insurance
  • Occupational pension
  • Survivor's pension
  • Repayment protection (återbetalningsskydd)
  • Employer death benefit
  • Collective agreement benefits
  • Salary owed
  • Holiday pay
  • Other employee benefits

Ask HR for a written breakdown of all benefits and amounts.

11. Pension

Check all of the following:

  • Collectum
  • Occupational pension
  • ITP
  • Pension providers
  • Repayment protection
  • Survivor protection
  • Any pension in India
Important question: "Did the deceased have återbetalningsskydd?" If yes, pension savings may be paid to eligible beneficiaries.

12. Family, Children & Support

If there is a child/family in Sweden, inform:

  • School / preschool
  • Social services (if the family needs support)
  • Försäkringskassan
  • Employer of surviving spouse
  • Healthcare / child support services

Ask what support is available for: childcare, financial support, survivor benefits, housing, psychological/grief support, and practical help.

Source: Försäkringskassan

13. Migrationsverket — Immigration Status

If the deceased was an Indian citizen/resident (not a Swedish citizen), check the family's immigration situation:

  • Surviving spouse's residence permit
  • Child's residence permit
  • Permanent residence status
  • Citizenship application
  • Any pending immigration case
  • Dependency / family connection

Inform Migrationsverket where the death affects a pending residence/family case.

Source: Migrationsverket

14. Dödsbo — The Estate

After death, everything owned and owed becomes part of the dödsbo (estate):

  • Bank accounts, savings, shares
  • Car, apartment/house, personal belongings
  • Insurance payments, pension-related assets
  • Loans, credit cards, mortgage, taxes, bills

The spouse, children, and other eligible heirs may be dödsbodelägare (estate beneficiaries).

If the deceased has very limited assets (insufficient beyond funeral costs), the family may use a dödsboanmälan instead of a full bouppteckning — handled by the socialnämnden in the municipality.

15. Bouppteckning (Estate Inventory)

This is one of the most important legal steps. It records assets, debts, spouse, children, and other estate beneficiaries.

  • Must be prepared within 3 months.
  • Must be submitted to Skatteverket within 4 months of death.
  • A registered bouppteckning is the formal proof of who can represent the estate — often needed by banks.

Source: Skatteverket — Bouppteckning

16. Recommended Timeline

🚨 First few hours

  • Contact healthcare/doctor (expected death) or 112 (unexpected).
  • Do not move the body unnecessarily.
  • Hospital handles documentation if death occurred there.
  • Ask for time with the deceased / family viewing.

🟠 First 24 hours

  • Contact funeral home.
  • Contact closest family.
  • Contact employer / HR.
  • Contact bank.
  • Contact insurance.
  • Secure home / car / property.
  • Locate passport, ID, insurance, and important documents.
  • If body may go to India — tell funeral home NOW.

🟡 First 2–7 days

  • Confirm Skatteverket registration.
  • Obtain dödsfallsintyg med släktutredning.
  • Funeral/cremation planning.
  • Contact Indian Embassy if Indian citizen.
  • Start insurance claims.
  • Contact pension providers.
  • Contact school/preschool.
  • Contact Migrationsverket if relevant.
  • Discuss financial/social support for surviving spouse and child.

🟢 Within 1 month

  • Cremation/burial (normally within 1 month).
  • Complete funeral arrangements.
  • Obtain cremation certificate.
  • If ashes going to India, start Embassy/transport documentation.

🔵 Within 3–4 months

  • Prepare bouppteckning within 3 months.
  • Submit to Skatteverket within 4 months.
  • Continue estate/bank/insurance administration.

17. Important "Do NOT" Checklist

Until the bank/estate representative advises otherwise:
  • ❌ Don't use the deceased person's bank card.
  • ❌ Don't withdraw/transfer money to another account.
  • ❌ Don't sell major assets privately.
  • ❌ Don't distribute inheritance yet.
  • ❌ Don't throw away documents.
  • ❌ Don't cancel insurance before checking claims.
Instead:
  • ✅ Contact the bank → establish the dödsbo.
  • ✅ Identify beneficiaries / debts.
  • ✅ Pay legitimate estate expenses.
  • ✅ Complete bouppteckning.
  • ✅ Then handle inheritance (arvskifte).

19. Religious Rituals & Sacred Items in Sweden

Finding religious ritual items in Sweden can be challenging. Below is guidance for different faiths. If you have additional information, please share it in the comments section below.

🙏 Hindu Rituals

  • Ganga Jal (Gangā water): May be available at Indian grocery stores in Stockholm, Malmö, or Gothenburg. Ask at temples or community organizations. Some families bring it from India and keep a small quantity at home.
  • Tulsi (holy basil): Can be grown at home or purchased at Indian stores.
  • Ghee, sandalwood, camphor, incense: Available at Indian grocery stores and some Asian supermarkets.
  • White cloth (kafan/shroud): Plain white cotton fabric from fabric stores.
  • Hindu priest (pandit): Contact local Hindu temples or the Indian community network in your city.

Temples in Sweden:

  • Hindu Mandir Stockholm
  • Hindu Cultural Centre, Gothenburg
  • Contact your local Indian association for temple/priest referrals

☪️ Muslim Rituals

  • Zamzam water: Available at some Islamic centres, halal stores, or through community members who have performed Hajj/Umrah.
  • Kafan (burial shroud): White cotton cloth, available at Islamic centres or fabric stores. Some mosques keep kafan in stock.
  • Ghusl (ritual washing): Many mosques in Sweden have facilities for ghusl. Contact your local mosque.
  • Imam / religious leader: Contact your local mosque or Islamic cultural centre.
  • Muslim burial: Several Swedish cemeteries have designated Muslim burial sections. Contact your municipality's kyrkogårdsförvaltning (cemetery administration).

Islamic centres in Sweden:

  • Stockholms Moské (Stockholm Grand Mosque)
  • Islamic Centre, Malmö
  • Göteborgs Moské
  • Contact Sveriges Muslimska Råd / Islamiska Förbundet for local mosque referrals

🙏 Sikh Rituals

  • Amrit (holy water): Available at Gurdwaras.
  • Rumala, Gutka Sahib, and other items: Ask at the local Gurdwara.
  • Granthi / religious leader: Contact the local Gurdwara.
  • Cremation: Sikhs traditionally cremate. Swedish crematoriums can accommodate this.

Gurdwaras in Sweden:

  • Gurdwara Singh Sabha, Stockholm
  • Gurdwara Malmö
  • Contact the local Sikh community for referrals

✝️ Christian Rituals

  • Holy water, Bible, crucifix: Available through local churches.
  • Last rites / priest: Contact your local church (Svenska kyrkan, Catholic church, or other denomination).
  • Hospital chaplains are often available through the hospital.

🙏 Jain Rituals

  • Sacred items: Contact the Indian community network — Jain communities in Sweden are small but connected.
  • Cremation: Jains traditionally cremate. Swedish crematoriums can accommodate this.

☸️ Buddhist Rituals

  • Incense, prayer beads, Buddhist texts: Available at Buddhist centres or Asian stores.
  • Monk / religious leader: Contact Buddhist centres in Stockholm, Gothenburg, or Malmö.
Can't find a ritual item? Contact your local Indian/South Asian community association, temple, mosque, or gurdwara. Many community members keep sacred items at home and are willing to help in times of need. Also check Indian grocery stores and online shops that ship to Sweden.
