Rest in Peace

— A compassionate guide for families in Sweden —

A practical step-by-step guide for Indian nationals and families living in Sweden. Covers Swedish authorities, Indian Embassy, cremation, repatriation, estate, insurance, religious rituals, and more.

⚠ Disclaimer: This guide is for general informational purposes only. It is not legal advice. Always verify with the relevant authorities. Last reviewed: August 2026.

Quick Priority Checklist

  1. Doctor/Hospital → Death confirmation
  2. Skatteverket → Death registered (doctor handles)
  3. Funeral company → Body, viewing, cremation
  4. Bank → Protect account, pay necessary bills
  5. Insurance + HR → Life insurance / pension
  6. Indian Embassy → Register death / passport
  7. School + social services → Child/family support
  8. Migrationsverket → Immigration if applicable
  9. Cremation → Skatteverket certificate + ceremony
  10. Ashes → Embassy docs if taking to India
  11. Bouppteckning → 3 months / submit within 4
  12. Inheritance → After estate administration

A. Death at home

Expected death (serious illness)

  • Contact the healthcare provider / home healthcare team.
  • A doctor must formally confirm the death.
  • The doctor handles documentation; funeral home takes care of the body.

Source: 1177 Vårdguiden

Unexpected or suspicious death

  • Call 112.
  • Do not move or disturb the body.
  • Police may investigate before the body is released.

B. Death in hospital

Hospital confirms death, prepares documentation, notifies Skatteverket, helps family say goodbye, contacts funeral home or faith representative.

Source: Skatteverket

The medical team usually starts the Swedish registration process automatically.

What happens:

  • Doctor/hospital sends the dödsbevis to Skatteverket.
  • Family usually does not need to manually file this first notice.
  • After registration, estate-related documents become available.

Dödsfallsintyg med släktutredning

This document is commonly required for practical next steps.

Used by:

  • Banks
  • Insurance companies
  • Pension providers
  • Other authorities

Source: Skatteverket

Contact a begravningsbyrå (funeral home) as early as possible.

They can help with:

  • Transportation of the deceased
  • Viewing arrangements
  • Religious or secular ceremony coordination
  • Cremation or burial process
  • Cremation and related certificates
  • Urn/ashes handling and municipality coordination
  • Administrative documents

Cremation rules

  • Skatteverket must issue a certificate before cremation/burial (funeral home usually handles this).
  • Cremation or burial is normally done within 1 month.
  • Ashes are usually interred within 1 year.

Source: Skatteverket — Begravning

Two parallel processes:

Swedish process

Death registered in Sweden first. Full Swedish process applies.

Indian Embassy

Death must also be registered with the Embassy of India, Stockholm.

Embassy documents

  1. Miscellaneous / Attestation form
  2. Original passport of deceased + applicant
  3. Copies of both passports
  4. Original Swedish death certificate + copy
  5. Payment proof — SEK 224
  6. Return envelope if applying by post

Useful consular checks:

  • Ask how deceased passport/OCI should be handled in this case.
  • Ask if additional documents are needed for local circumstances.

Same-day processing when complete. Embassy — Death Certificate →

Emergency (death): +46 73 484 4992 · Consular: +46 8 684 32100 — Contact →

Flow:

  • Death in Sweden
  • Swedish registration completed
  • Funeral home and cremation process
  • Cremation certificate issued
  • Embassy documentation for transport
  • Ashes carried/sent to India

Embassy documents for ashes

  • Death Certificate + Cremation Certificate
  • Undertaker certificate (urn sealed)
  • Passport/OCI of deceased + carrier (originals + copies)
  • Embassy payment proof

Embassy — Taking Ashes to India →

IMPORTANT: If the family plans final rites in India, inform hospital/funeral home immediately and do not approve cremation in Sweden.
Suggested sentence: "The family has decided to send the body to India. Please do not proceed with cremation."

Step 1: Inform hospital or funeral home immediately

  • State that you want to send the body to India.
  • Pause all cremation decisions until the family confirms repatriation.
  • If cremation is completed in Sweden, only ashes can be transported.

Step 2: Appoint an international funeral home

Choose a funeral provider experienced in international repatriation.

They usually coordinate:

  • Collection of the body and mortuary storage
  • Embalming and zinc-lined coffin preparation
  • Swedish documentation and authority certificates
  • Airline cargo booking and airport handling
  • Customs documentation and coordination with India-side services

Official coordinator in Sweden

  • Swedish Funeral International Assistance (SFIA) operated by SBF: international transport, documentation, Embassy and airline coordination, and body preparation.

SFIA information (SBF) →

Funeral providers to ask for repatriation support

  • FussNord AB International Funeral Assistance (24-hour assistance)
  • Ignis Begravningsbyra
  • Evigo begravningsbyra AB
  • Sveriges Auktoriserade Begravnings- & Juridikbyraer (SBF member network)
  • Jensen Funeral Service (ask whether they manage directly or through SFIA/partner)

Step 3: Contact Embassy of India (Stockholm)

The Embassy supports death registration, consular paperwork, passport cancellation, and transport documentation for body or ashes.

  • Death registration
  • Mortal remains / ashes information
  • Contact details

Step 4: Obtain required Swedish documents

Funeral homes usually help gather and sequence these documents:

  • Swedish death certificate and registration confirmation
  • Permission for transport
  • Balsameringsintyg (embalming certificate)
  • Smittointyg (non-contagious disease certificate)
  • Lodningsintyg / coffin sealing certificate
  • Transportintyg and passport copy
  • Airline cargo documents

If police or forensic review is required, transport can proceed only after authorities release the body.

Step 5: Airline cargo booking

  • The funeral home normally books shipment as air cargo.
  • Families usually do not book this directly with the airline.
  • The provider coordinates cargo terminal, customs, airline handling, and India receiving arrangements.

Step 6: Receiving the body in India

  • Receiving funeral home or family representative collects from the destination airport.
  • Customs clearance is completed in India.
  • Ambulance/hearse transfer and final rites are arranged locally.

Examples of India-side providers (verify independently): Beyond Life Services, Swargayatraa.

Decision tip:

  • If family wants final cremation in India, decide early and pause Swedish cremation steps.
  • Ask two written quotations: full body repatriation vs cremation + ashes transfer.

The bank step should be handled very carefully because the money belongs to the dödsbo (estate).

Do NOT:
  • Use the deceased person's debit card.
  • Withdraw money for personal use.
  • Transfer money to another account without legal authority.
  • Empty the account.
Do:
  • Contact the bank as soon as possible and book a meeting.
  • Inform them: "The account holder has passed away. Please guide us on legal estate handling and urgent bill payments."
  • Ask how funeral and urgent household bills can be paid through estate rules.
  • Ask what documents are required from spouse/family representatives.

Source: Skatteverket

Insurance should be contacted early, ideally in the first 2-3 days.

Contact:

  • Life insurance
  • Home insurance
  • Accident insurance
  • Employer insurance
  • Pension insurance
  • Union insurance (if applicable)
  • Credit / mortgage insurance
  • Any private insurance in India

Request written confirmation for:

  • Policy number
  • Beneficiary
  • Amount payable
  • Required documents
  • Expected processing timeline

If the person was employed, contact HR immediately and ask for a complete written benefit summary.

Ask HR to verify:

  • Group life insurance
  • Occupational pension and survivor pension
  • Repayment protection (återbetalningsskydd)
  • Employer death benefit
  • Collective agreement benefits
  • Salary owed
  • Holiday pay
  • Any additional employee benefits

Ask for written details of each amount, recipient, and expected payout date.

Review all pension channels as early as possible.

Check with:

  • Collectum
  • ITP / occupational pension providers
  • Private pension providers
  • Any pension accounts in India

Verify whether the pension has:

  • Repayment protection
  • Survivor protection
  • Named beneficiaries
Key question: "Did the deceased have återbetalningsskydd?" If yes, savings may be paid to eligible survivors.

If there is a child/family in Sweden, inform:

  • School / preschool
  • Social services (if the family needs support)
  • Försäkringskassan
  • Employer of surviving spouse
  • Healthcare / child support services

Ask what support is available for:

  • Childcare
  • Financial support
  • Survivor benefits
  • Housing
  • Psychological / grief support
  • Practical day-to-day help

Source: Försäkringskassan

If the deceased was an Indian citizen, immigration status may need review for the surviving family.

Check:

  • Residence permit of surviving spouse
  • Residence permit of children
  • Permanent residence status
  • Citizenship applications
  • Any pending migration/family reunification case

Inform Migrationsverket where the death affects an ongoing case.

Source: Migrationsverket

After death, everything owned and owed becomes part of the dödsbo (estate).

This can include:

  • Bank accounts, savings, shares
  • Car, house/apartment, personal belongings
  • Insurance and pension-related payouts
  • Loans, credit cards, mortgage, taxes, and bills

The spouse, children, and other eligible heirs are usually dödsbodelägare.

If the estate has very limited assets, a dödsboanmälan may replace a full bouppteckning, handled by the municipality's socialnämnd.

Bouppteckning is one of the most important legal steps for the estate.

It documents:

  • Assets
  • Debts
  • Spouse / children / other beneficiaries
  • Who is authorized to represent the estate

Deadlines:

  • Prepare within 3 months of death
  • Submit to Skatteverket within 4 months

A registered bouppteckning is formal proof often required by banks and authorities.

Source: Skatteverket — Bouppteckning

🚨 First hours

  • Healthcare/doctor or 112 depending on case.
  • Do not move the body unnecessarily.

🟠 First 24 hours

  • Contact funeral home, close family, HR, bank, and insurance.
  • Secure home/car and gather ID/passport/documents.
  • Body to India? Inform funeral home immediately.

🟡 Days 2-7

  • Confirm Skatteverket registration and obtain dödsfallsintyg.
  • Plan ceremony and contact Embassy/pension/school/Migrationsverket as relevant.

🟢 Within 1 month

  • Complete cremation or burial.
  • Start ashes-transfer documents if applicable.

🔵 Months 3-4

  • Bouppteckning prepared (within 3 months) and submitted (within 4 months).
Don't:
  • Use the deceased person's card or move money informally.
  • Sell major assets privately.
  • Distribute inheritance before estate process is complete.
  • Throw away legal/financial documents.
  • Cancel insurance before checking claim rights.
Do:
  • Bank meeting and legal estate setup.
  • Track debts/claims and preserve documentation.
  • Complete bouppteckning before inheritance distribution.
